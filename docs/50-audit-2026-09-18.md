# Infraque — audit synthesis, consolidated spec, and the plan forward (2026-09-18)

Written by the coordinator from sixteen independent review reports commissioned on 2026-09-18 (five
code audits: pipeline, database and API, web, infrastructure, document coherence; eleven specialists:
design, front-end, backend, architecture, data science, QA, operations, legal, product, market,
commercial, editorial) plus the live verification of the ADR 0008 lanes on the same day. Every number
below was measured by a reviewer or by the coordinator; inferences are marked. The raw reports are in
the session transcript, not the repo; their findings are carried here by area with the file they cite.

This document is the project's memory for what was found and what was agreed. `docs/00-PLAN.md`'s
decisions log records the owner's decisions; this file records the state and the sequence.

---

## 1. Verdict in one paragraph

The platform is real, disciplined where discipline is expressible in code, and not launchable as it
sits. What holds: provenance on every row, licence gating at ingest in three places, a single
visibility predicate, enforced layering, strict typing, 1,334 core and 166 web tests green, a legal
register that quotes operative clauses, and a commercial design (Attio and Stripe behind ports) that
the code honours. What does not: the infrastructure has never been executed and would not deploy; the
ingestion loop stops at the filesystem; the change-event stream, which is the paid product, fabricates
and drops events under reproducible conditions; several read paths re-implement the visibility
predicate and leak; the site has no way to take money or send a lawful email; the manifest records
fourteen unread-terms sources as publishable; and the product documents describe a proposals-only
product the owner replaced on 2026-09-18. None of this argues for a rewrite (four reviewers were asked
and all four said no, with evidence). It argues for a fixed sequence: blockers, shop, ownership graph
and pages, then measured waves.

## 2. Consolidated spec (supersedes the scattered decisions; the plan's rows remain the record)

**What Infraque is.** A free, cited, US-registry-grade map of energy infrastructure with owners, and
crawlable asset and company pages, as the acquisition surface; and a paid change feed of proposals
(supply) and opportunities (demand) with alerts, exports and an API as the product. The
differentiation, corrected by the market review: *no free product joins US registry assets, owner
shares and dated change events to public demand*. The join itself is offered by GEM's ownership
tracker (free, global, shallow on US generators) and by Enverus and S&P (enterprise-priced). What
nobody sells is the cross-source, dated status-change feed joined to demand.

**Placement grades (owner, 2026-09-18).** A proposal is drawn only at its true grade: `exact` as a
point, joinable to assets; `region` (county, state or country precision) as the highlighted polygon
carrying a count, never a point, never joined to an asset; `none` in list, search and alerts only,
with the reason on the record. Measured on the ADR 0008 dev load (5,837 active proposals): exact 2,341
(40%), county 2,235 and state 15 (39%), unknown 1,246 (21%).

**Assets are first-class (ADR 0008).** Twelve asset types on one table with public ids, slugs, owner
and operator edges with shares and as-of dates, GLEIF parent links, objective attributes only. No
valuation, tariff or contract data (owner). Assets get a narrow event vocabulary (status, owner,
attributes changed) in the next wave so ownership changes become alerts and page content.

**Tiers.** Free: delayed proposals and opportunities, the whole asset map and all asset and company
pages. Pro: live rows, saved searches, alerts, exports. API: metered access, webhooks, licence
envelope. Team: to be decided (§5). The paywall moves from *time* to *shape* for everything except ISO
change events, where the lag stays (product review; owner decision pending, §5).

**Buyers, in revenue order (commercial review).** Co-op and G&T group licences; lenders and funds;
developers and IPPs; data-centre operators; EPC and OEM; advisory and law as channels; midstream, RNG
and ethanol via three discovery conversations before any build.

**Out of scope.** Private aggregators, unlicensed ISO rows, valuation, matching and intake until a
paying customer asks, X as a channel until a measured click-through justifies it.

**Launch-ready means.** All §3.1 blockers closed with tests; a first deploy rehearsed on staging with a
rollback and a restore; the go/no-go checklist rows each carrying a command and its output.

## 3. State, by severity

### 3.1 Launch blockers (must close before any public deploy)

Infrastructure and operations (infra audit, ops review; all verified by rendering the files):
- No service declares an env file, so every secret except the database URL is dropped before it reaches a container; the session secret then falls back to a constant committed in the repo.
- The deploy script pulls images nobody builds or pushes and runs migrations against an unsynced host; the base Compose file is an invalid project; the backup is never scheduled and its tools never installed; no error tracking or metrics code exists; connector retries fire five times with zero backoff; the run table and health column that every freshness alert depends on have no writer on the scheduled path; structured log fields are discarded at emission.
- The always-on loop is not a loop: the scheduler ends at fetching; nothing loads, resolves or enriches (architecture review). Capacity at ten times the sources is thirty-four hours per daily burst (ops review).

Change events (pipeline audit, reproduced end to end):
- A row reorder in a source file fabricates withdrawals and swaps identities (positional `#2` suffix); live on NYISO.
- A status that returns to a previous value is silently dropped (idempotency key omits time and before-state); a record removed twice is recorded removed once.
- A corrupt download escapes the runner, leaves no run record and aborts the rest of the nightly batch.
- A Cloudflare challenge served as 403 is retried instead of reported blocked; robots rules naming our bot are ignored; field provenance dates never update after the first load.

Licence and privacy (legal and coherence audits, database audit):
- Fourteen register-`unknown` sources are recorded `attribution` in the manifest and are therefore publishable raw on the free tier; the derived-only rule is a regex over free-text notes; the asset endpoint had no licence gate; exact coordinates publish under licences that forbid raw.
- The events feed and Pro digest emails bypass the record-level visibility gate and leak names, URLs and transitions of hidden and restricted records.
- Erasure writes the erased email and name into the append-only audit log and never clears the deletion task's contact; the privacy notice promises a filer-deletion route that does not exist; no suppression store.
- The pricing copy promises a lag schedule the code cannot represent; Team is five times Pro for identical access; no legal entity, a placeholder postal address, no unsubscribe headers, so no email can be sent lawfully.

Fixed in this session's landing commit (tests added for each): public cache header on credentialed responses; junk-credential rate-limit bypass; DOM injection in the drawer and tooltips; open redirect via backslash; the site's own calls sharing the anonymous bucket (service token); null-sort cursor 500; slug resolution for asset and company pages; the order-dependent logging failure; 21 MB of stale public data dumps.

### 3.2 Important, not blocking

- Model identifiers in 139 of 140 commit messages (repo guardrail); rewrite history before the repo is public or accept it as internal; commit-message hook either way. Owner decision (§5).
- API structure: one 1,564-line module plus four admin modules of 1,000 to 1,500 lines, six helpers copied between them, no request models, no logger, three different request ids per response; the concrete split is in the backend review and is the refactor budget.
- Map work in Python over whole-table caches instead of PostGIS; move before the second asset type.
- Docket linkage capped by retrieval (full-text off, three-page cap), not by matching; recall unidentified by the evaluation design; three organisation keys in three modules (one fixed today).
- Web: no error state on the map, no page renders without JavaScript at the root, no Open Graph or structured data, contradictory performance budget, four template environments, a 988-line map script with no unit tests, region-grade records unreachable by keyboard, chip contrast 4.47:1 and control borders 1.36:1.
- Publishing: two templates can publish the literal word "None"; the automation disclosure names the old company; digests lack a postal address and the delayed notice; no record page has an alert sign-up; RSS items link to the homepage.
- Documents: the PRD, IA and pricing copy predate the 2026-09-18 decisions; the architecture document was last edited 2026-09-12; the decisions log has become the project; two duplicate document numbers; the old name survives in three files.

### 3.3 Strengths to leave alone

The visibility predicate where it is called; the connector framework and its gates; the provenance quartet; the identifier-free interaction counters (a "no tracking, no banner" claim that is true); the status harmonisation map; the ports-and-adapters boundary; the legal register's method; the CI breadth once its stubbed gates are switched on.

## 4. Plan, in order, with gates

Each item names the reviewer whose findings define its acceptance. Estimates are the coordinator's, not measured, unless stated.

1. **Merge PR #1** (owner). Green on every head since 2026-09-15.
2. **Blockers sprint, one to two weeks.** Infrastructure (env files, session secret, image build and push to a registry, deploy order, Compose validity, backup timer, error tracking, retry backoff, run and health writers, JSON logging); the load and resolve jobs so the loop closes; the five change-event defects with reproducing tests; the manifest reuse classes aligned to the register with a CI check; explicit `publication_rule` field; asset visibility filter; events and digest visibility; coordinate downgrade under raw-forbidding licences; erasure and suppression; "None" template gate and disclosure constant; digest address and notice. Gate: every item has a test; CI gates that are stubbed today (spec byte-for-byte, coverage floor with the visibility predicate at 100%, evaluation thresholds) switched on.
3. **The shop, one sprint.** Pricing page from the existing copy, upgrade button calling checkout, saved-search and alert screens, "alert me on this view" on map and list, alert sign-up on every record page, RSS items fixed. Team sold as seats plus support until export and watchlists exist, or held. Metric: self-served paid seats.
4. **Ownership graph and company pages, one to two weeks.** Load EIA-860 owner shares (the parquet is built; the loader is landed and keyed on the corp-suffix-stripping organisation key), GLEIF parents behind a 200-pair labelled sample, as-of and source on every ownership row, a corrections route with a published SLA, provenance on organisations, attribution line on company pages, index pages and navigation for assets and companies, Open Graph and structured data, robots.txt. This is the Tallgrass conversation asset: a public page, not a message.
5. **Proposal location quality, in parallel.** Re-geocode on update, fix the six county and city collisions, promote exact points when a source later supplies them, publish the placement split on the about page.
6. **Twenty discovery conversations (owner).** Three with midstream, RNG or ethanol contacts, shown a company page and asked what they would stop paying for; the answers gate the midstream wave. Also the five-dollar experiments the market review lists: one month of the competitor's starter tier, twenty search-rank queries, three screenshot conversations.
7. **Refactor budget, alongside 3 and 4.** Split the API by resource into packages with a shared query layer; one templating module; the map script into seven modules with unit tests; PostGIS for bbox, clustering and radius before the second asset type.
8. **Permit events sprint**, then **midstream and fuels layers** behind the corrected engagement gate (a rate, not a count: page-view denominator and a layers prop on alert events), then the **county pilot** under its recorded kill criteria. Gas capacity in the queue rose 86% in 2025 while every renewable class fell (LBNL 2026), which is the stronger argument for the gas layers than any conversation; record it as a second trigger.
9. **Ninety-day operations plan** (ops review, adopted as written): instrument first (days 1 to 15), automate the deploy and rehearse rollback and restore (16 to 30), safety net and dashboards (31 to 60), measure the freshness promise and load-test at ten times sources (61 to 90).

## 5. Decisions the owner must make (the audits surfaced these; the coordinator will not decide them)

1. **Commit history.** Rewrite the 139 commits carrying model identifiers before the repo is ever public, or accept the history as internal and add the hook. Cheap now, impossible after launch.
2. **Paywall shape.** Time (the current delay) or shape (alerts, exports, API, watchlists) with the lag kept only on ISO change events. The product and commercial reviews both recommend shape.
3. **Team tier.** Ship as seats plus support at the current price, hold it until export and watchlists exist, or cut it.
4. **Counsel order.** The legal review's revised list puts privacy and erasure first, PJM second, customer terms third, the fourteen unread EU and other sources sixth. Confirm, and confirm which items block launch versus first sale.
5. **Legal entity and postal address.** Open since 2026-09-12; now blocks every outbound email, Stripe, Attio and customer terms.
6. **Segment slots.** Three of twenty discovery conversations for midstream, RNG and ethanol, or none until the ownership graph ships.
7. **Refactor pace.** The §4.7 budget alongside feature work, or deferred until after the shop ships.
8. **The engagement gate restated as a rate**, with a number: the coordinator proposes a company-or-asset-page-to-registration rate of two percent as the signal that the map is marketing rather than decoration.
