# UX and information-architecture review, 2026-10-20

**Status:** review, v1 · 2026-10-20 · author: product-designer · for: owner and coordinator
**Asked by:** owner, after using the live beta: "The map outline on the site isn't rendering ... Could we make all the
pages organized a bit better? Currently, they are long scrolling pages. Could we reprioritize what appears first, put
companies on top of such pages ... add tabs ... I LOVE the company pipelines. But could we add to it their current
assets as well? Also the difference between the main page tabs, like proposals vs opportunities are a bit confusing.
Could we combine some of these tabs ..."
**Judged against:** `docs/04-standards.md` §2 (D-1…D-33), `docs/30-design-references.md` §3 synthesis,
`docs/30-design-ia.md`, `docs/31-design-system.md`.
**Scope:** public surface only. Admin is out of scope. This document recommends; it changes no code.

## 0. Summary: the five changes that matter most

1. **The map shows no land, water or borders because a fallback layer is painted over, not only because a file is
   missing (§2).** The missing `basemap.pmtiles` is known and fixed by the next deploy. Separately, the site already
   ships a same-origin outline (country fill, coastline, state lines) for exactly this case, but in production
   (PMTiles) mode it is hidden. The Protomaps style's first layer is an opaque `background` layer with no source, and
   it paints over the outline whether or not the tiles load. Reproduced locally with the file answering 404: the
   result matches the owner's description, data on a blank beige plane. Removing that one layer brings the outline
   back. One line in `web/static/js/basemap.js`; it also fixes the company and asset mini-maps.
2. **Put the company at the top of every record and on every list (§4.2, §4.4).** Today no proposal page, proposal
   list row or map drawer links to its company. The sponsor is plain text in row five of the field grid
   (`web/viewmodels.py:636` keeps only the name, though the API sends the slug). Search for "NextEra" lists Companies
   last, at 6,715 px. The Companies index opens on "1000 Mile Solar, LLC" and "11601 Copenhagen Ct, LLC".
3. **Add operating assets to the company pipeline as columns in the tables the owner likes (§4.1, §5).** The store
   already names each plant's operator, but no power plant is linked to a company as operator (0 of 16,472). So
   Invenergy's page says "Owns 8 power plants", while the store has 70 plants (12.8 GW) whose EIA-860M operator is
   Invenergy Services LLC. Duke's page shows 51 plants against 155 (66.5 GW) operated by Duke subsidiaries. The fix
   has two parts: operator edges for EIA-860M plants (data lane), and an **Operating** column beside **Active
   pipeline** in the by-technology and by-ISO tables (design).
4. **Give company pages a summary header and URL tabs, and give other record pages a summary header plus a sticky
   section bar (§3.2, §4).** NextEra's page is 9,959 px at 1440 wide (11 screens) and 17,223 px at 400 wide (21
   screens). Its own proposal list starts at 4,621 px. The proposed Overview tab fits in about two screens, and each
   long list moves to its own tab with its own URL.
5. **Six top-level links instead of ten, with names that say supply versus demand (§3.1).** The links become Map ·
   Projects · Companies · RFPs & funding · Infrastructure · Alerts. Assets and Grid connection points merge under
   Infrastructure; About moves to the masthead. Proposals and Opportunities stay separate, renamed. Part of the
   confusion is content rather than labels: 483 of the 624 open opportunities (77%) are EU TED tenders, and the
   default list opens on three military burn-injury research grants. The renamed tab needs an energy-relevant default
   (§4.5).

## 2. The map: why the physical map does not show

**Known cause, not a finding:** `basemap.pmtiles` is missing from the tiles bucket until the next deploy promotes it.

**Finding M-1: the fallback outline is masked in PMTiles mode.** `web/static/js/basemap.js` starts every map from a
same-origin outline style (`fallbackStyle`: water background, country fill, country and state lines from
`/static/data/basemap_fallback.geojson`). Its comment calls this "what the user sees whenever the real basemap fails".
On `load`, `addPmtilesBasemap` appends every layer that `basemaps.layers("protomaps", ...)` returns. The first of these
is `{id: "background", type: "background"}`, which needs no source and fills the whole canvas with `--map-land`. When
the tiles fail, every source-backed Protomaps layer draws nothing, but this one still draws and covers the outline.
`onFail` only sends a `map.basemap_failed` UI event; it removes nothing.

- *Reproduced:* `MAP_TILE_URL=https://tiles.audit.invalid/basemap.pmtiles`, with that URL answering 404 in the
  browser. The home map shows clusters and pipelines on a uniform beige plane with no coast, water or state lines.
- *Fix verified in the browser only:* the same page with `styleLayers.filter(l => l.type !== "background")` shows the
  outline (water, coast, state lines) under the data. When the tiles do load, Protomaps' `earth` and `water` fills cover
  the outline, so the working map is unchanged.
- *Same bug on the mini-maps:* company and asset pages use the same `Basemap.addBasemap` through `asset_map.js`.
- *Test to add:* in `web/test_map_layers.py` or the e2e suite, route the PMTiles URL to 404. Then assert that no
  sourceless layer sits above `fallback-land` in `map.getStyle().layers`, or sample an ocean pixel for `--map-water`.
- *Also:* on failure, show a one-line caption under the map ("Base map unavailable; showing state outlines"). Without
  it, a degraded map reads as a broken site.

**Checked and not blocking:** the CSP allows `{$MAP_TILE_URL}` in `connect-src` and `img-src`, the Protomaps
glyphs and sprites, and `worker-src blob:`. The pinned `pmtiles@4.5.0` and `@protomaps/basemaps@5.7.2` scripts load
with SRI. Deploy sets R2 CORS for `https://{DOMAIN}` (`infra/ci/beta.sh`), and `www.` redirects to the apex
(`infra/compose/Caddyfile`). A future `staging.` host would need its own origin in that CORS rule.

**Other map findings:**

- **M-2: at 400 px the open Key covers about half of the 464 px map** (`home-400-fold`). Collapse the Key by default
  below 720 px, as the filter bar already collapses.
- **M-3: Duke's page has no map at all:** "No map: 49 assets across 4 companies is past the 40 this page can draw".
  MapLibre draws thousands of points; 40 is a layout choice, not a limit. Cluster the points and draw the whole group.
- **M-4: the map drawer cannot name the company**, because `/v1/proposals/geo` features carry no sponsor (see
  `web/static/js/map.js` line 1239's comment). This is part of §4.2.
- `/favicon.ico` answers 404 on every page (a console error on each load). Add the icon.

## 3. Navigation and page model

### 3.1 Top navigation: recommendation

Today the bar has ten links plus search: Map · Proposals · Opportunities · Assets · Grid connection points · Companies
· About & sources · (Pricing) · Alerts · Sign in. At 1440 it fills the header row; below 720 px it collapses behind
Menu. Three problems:

- **"Proposals" and "Opportunities" are near-synonyms** in plain English, so the supply/demand distinction (the core of
  `docs/00-PLAN.md`) is invisible in the labels. Each list explains itself in a paragraph ("Supply-side records ...",
  "Demand-side records ..."), which a first-time visitor reads only after clicking.
- **Two links lead to the same kind of thing.** "Assets" and "Grid connection points" both answer "what already exists
  here, and where would I connect?". They are reference layers rather than the product's live records (00-PLAN,
  2026-09-18: assets and company pages are the acquisition surface; proposals and opportunities are the product).
- **About & sources is reference material** sitting among destinations.

**Recommendation: six primary links, in this order, with routes unchanged.**

| # | Today | Proposed label | Route (unchanged) | Page H1 and one-line standfirst | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Map | **Map** | `/` | unchanged | Primary surface (D-8) |
| 2 | Proposals | **Projects** | `/proposals` | "Projects in development". Supply side: grid-queue requests, permits and planned plants | "Project" is the word developers, investors and utilities use. The data model and API keep `proposal`. |
| 3 | Companies | **Companies** (moved up from 6th) | `/organizations` | unchanged | The join between projects, RFPs and assets, and the owner's most-used surface |
| 4 | Opportunities | **RFPs & funding** | `/opportunities` | "RFPs, tenders and funding". Demand side: buyers and funders asking for projects | Says what the rows are. Supply versus demand becomes "projects" versus "buyers and funders". |
| 5 | Assets, Grid connection points | **Infrastructure**, with two tabs: *Plants, pipelines & lines* · *Grid connection points* | `/assets`, `/interconnection-points` | "Existing infrastructure" | One home for what already exists; the asset-type chips on `/assets` stay |
| 6 | Alerts | **Alerts** | `/alerts` | unchanged | |
| – | About & sources | masthead utility link beside the freshness line, plus the footer | `/about` | unchanged | Reference, not a destination |
| – | Pricing | off under `noncommercial` (unchanged) | `/pricing` | – | |

Search and Sign in stay at the right of the bar. Breadcrumbs follow the new labels (Home / Projects / …).

**Why not merge Projects and RFPs & funding into one tab.** The two record types share almost no columns or filters:
MW, queue, lifecycle state and connection point against deadline, issuer, budget and kind. A merged list would need
a type switch at the top, which is the same two tabs one level deeper. It would also pull the demand set's relevance
problem (§4.5) into the project list. The supply/demand distinction stays, as the plan requires; the labels now carry
it. The two meet where a combined view helps the reader: company pages (projects sponsored beside RFPs issued),
search (one results page) and, later, matches (`docs/00-PLAN.md` 2026-09-27: matches stay unpublished until they pass
their precision bar).

**Departure to record.** `docs/04-standards.md` D-16 still lists the original nav (Proposals · Opportunities · Map ·
Feed · Alerts · API), which is already out of date (`docs/30-design-ia.md` §1 records the as-built list). Update both
with this change, and the "Names" table in docs/30 §1.

### 3.2 Page model: tabs, a sticky section bar, or collapsing sections

There are three tools, each used where it fits. All of them stay within D-17 (every tab and view lives in the URL) and
D-18 (primary content is server-rendered HTML).

| Pattern | Where | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Summary header** | every record page (company, project, RFP, grid point, asset, site) | Name, status or type, **company line**, 3–4 key figures in a ruled strip, a one-line provenance statement ("From CAISO Public Queue Report and EIA-860M, retrieved 9 Oct 2026 · Sources"), Follow. The provenance line keeps D-2 (evidence one click away) when the full Sources panel moves down the page. |
| **URL tabs** (`?tab=`) | company pages, the Infrastructure pair, search | Plain links rendered as a tab bar with counts, in the GitHub repository idiom (summary above; "Issues 261 · Pull requests 34", each tab its own URL; https://github.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js, viewed 2026-10-20). Each tab is a server-rendered page, so it works with scripts off (D-30) and can hold a full, paged, sortable table rather than a 100-row list. Markup is `<nav aria-label="Company sections">` with `aria-current="page"`, not `role="tablist"`, because the tabs are navigations. |
| **Sticky section bar** | project, RFP, grid point, asset and site pages (1,100–2,400 px at 1440) | A 40 px bar of anchor links (`#facts`, `#timeline`, `#sources`) that sticks under the header. These pages are not long enough to split. |
| **Collapsed by default** | long secondary blocks: merged-record table over 3 rows, History over 5 events, Subsidiaries over 10 | `<details>` with a count in the summary, open when the URL fragment targets it |
| **Sticky table of contents** | `/about`, `/methodology` (10,894 px; 31,362 px at 400) | Left rail at ≥1080 px; an "On this page" disclosure below that |

Tab switches are full page loads, so D-15 needs no new motion rule. The sticky bar uses the paper background and a
bottom rule, with no shadow and no blur (D-7).

**Provenance on every tab.** A company tab shows only some of the page's rows, so each tab ends with the Sources for
the rows it shows, and the header's provenance line names every register behind the page. The rule that every screen
shows provenance and licence then holds per tab.

## 4. Per-page section order

"First-time reader" here means the three people in the brief: an energy developer, an investor and a utility analyst.
On a record, all three first ask what it is, who is behind it, how big it is, where it is, and how far along it is.
Only then do they want the evidence and history.

### 4.1 Company page (`/organizations/{slug}`), the largest change

**Today (NextEra, offsets at 1440):** H1 142 · summary line · Follow · fields grid (Type: Other, Country, Parent: "No
parent recorded …") · scope links · group line · **Pipeline 542** · Portfolio 951 · Subsidiaries 1,161 · **1. Assets
1,468** (map, then one table per role and type) · 2. Proposals near these assets 3,550 · **3. Proposals 4,621** (73
rows, about 4,700 px) · 4. Opportunities 9,330 ("No opportunities issued by this company.") · 5. Sources 9,445.

**Why the existing Assets section was missed.** The section exists; seven things hide it.

1. *It comes fourth.* It follows three blocks, two of them lists of companies. At 1440 it starts at 1,468 px (second
   screen); at 400 at 2,465 px (NextEra) and 2,376 px (Duke).
2. *The Pipeline block looks like the page summary.* It is the only block with headline numbers and tables, it covers
   proposals only, and it sits right under the header. A reader concludes the page is about proposals. Assets have no
   equivalent: no total MW and no breakdown by technology.
3. *Numbering restarts at "1. Assets".* Pipeline, Portfolio and Subsidiaries are unnumbered, so "1." reads as the start
   of an appendix.
4. *It opens with a map, and in production that map is blank* (§2, M-1). A blank panel signals "nothing here".
5. *The Portfolio table already answers "assets?"* ("Assets 25 / 8 / 1") and the eye moves on.
6. *The header summary is small grey text and leads with the trivial item* ("Operates 1 RNG project · Owns 33 power
   plants"), and it does not link to the section.
7. *When found, the tables do not answer the question.* They show no technology, status or commissioned year, so
   "what does NextEra run" (gas and nuclear in Florida) cannot be read. They are not sorted. They have one row per
   ownership edge, not per plant: Scherer and St Johns River Power Park appear twice, once from EIA-860 and once from
   GHGRP. The page gives four different asset counts: "Owns 33" in the header, "Owns 34" in the table heading, "33
   assets held by 3 companies" and "35 of 35 assets with a mapped location". Duke's gives "49 assets held by 4
   companies" against "Owns 51".

The deeper reason is coverage (§5): the section lists only plants with an *owner* edge, so most of each fleet is
absent.

**Proposed: summary header plus tabs.** Overview is the default tab. Target height: 2 screens at 1440, 4 at 400.

**Today:** buckets in fixed order, Proposals (all 50 inline, to 3,520 px) · Opportunities · Assets (50) · **Companies**

**Proposed:**

- A **tab bar with counts** (Companies 13 · Projects 50 · Assets 50 · RFPs 0), ordered by match type, so a name match

### 4.5 RFPs & funding (`/opportunities`) and its record

- **Default scope is the relevance problem, not the label.** Of 624 open notices, 483 are EU TED tenders, 103 are
  grants.gov, 26 are World Bank and 12 are Find a Tender. Only 307 match even a broad energy keyword set, and many of
  those are fuel purchases ("Diesel fuel", "Heating oil"). The default list opens on three "DoW Military Burn" research
  grants, tagged "all-source". Recommendation: a default view of energy-relevant notices, with an "All notices" switch
  (owner question 2, §7). Classification is a data-lane task.
- **Issuer as a name, not a code.** "DOD-AMRAA (agency code)" wraps to four lines (103 px rows). Show the agency name
  with the code in mono beside it, and link the issuer's company page where one exists.
- **Record header:** issuer, deadline with days remaining, kind and jurisdiction on the first two lines. Normalise
  dates: "Opens 2025-01-08" sits beside "Due 30 Nov 2026" on the same page.

## 5. Company assets beside the pipeline: what it takes

**Design:** add an Operating column to the existing Pipeline tables (§4.1). The owner likes those tables; the fleet
belongs inside them rather than in a separate block four screens down. GEM's Global Integrated Power Tracker publishes
exactly this shape, "Power Capacity by Technology and Status" (operating beside pipeline states, by technology;
https://globalenergymonitor.org/projects/global-integrated-power-tracker/, viewed 2026-10-20). Label the two sources
of truth in the tables: *Operating* from asset registers (EIA-860M, EIA-860, GHGRP), *pipeline* from queues and
permits. A built proposal and its operating plant can then be compared, not double-counted.

**Data (blocking for the numbers to be believable):** `asset_owner` holds 7,263 `owner` edges and 2,760 `operator`
edges. Every operator edge comes from the EIA Atlas midstream layers, ethanol and LMOP; **no power plant has one**.
Meanwhile every EIA-860M plant row carries `operator_name`. Matching on that name in the local store gives:

| Company page | Power plants shown today | Plants whose EIA-860M operator name matches | MW of those |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Invenergy LLC | 8 | 70 (`Invenergy Services LLC`) | 12,820 |
| Duke Energy Corp | 51 | 155 (Duke Energy Carolinas, Florida, Progress, Indiana, Kentucky) | 66,451 |
| NextEra Energy Inc (via FPL) | 34 | 144 (`Florida Power & Light Co`), plus 52 NextEra-named | 42,128 + 5,205 |

These are upper bounds from a name match, not resolved edges. The data lane should create `operator` edges for
EIA-860M plants through the same organisation resolver and alias rules the midstream operators use, so the plant counts
on these pages come from resolved edges, not name matches. The page then states roles honestly ("Operates 70 power
plants · owns shares in 8"). Until then, the Assets tab says what it covers: "Plants linked to this company so far,
from EIA-860 owner records and EPA GHGRP; plants it operates are not yet linked." The page already does this for parent
companies.
