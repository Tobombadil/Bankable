## 0. The answer in one paragraph

The platform is better engineered than most early-stage data companies at the things that are hard to retrofit:
- licence gating enforced in code and audited nightly;
- provenance on every record;
- reversible, evented merges;
- one error model;
- a tested deploy.

It is built for a buyer nobody has spoken to. Three things are true at once:
- **Breadth ran ahead of learning.** The code grew 66 % in 14 days, while the discovery conversations and pre-sales that were meant to gate that growth never happened.
- **The core product has correctness bugs.** The change feed currently announces completions and closures as withdrawals, and it serves ERCOT, 78 % of US queue rows, one release late.
- **The docs promise more than exists.** Across the docs the system describes protections it does not have: a generated API contract, a second factor on admin, per-service database roles, a Postgres rate limiter, alerting.

The next sprint should not add features. Do three things:
1. Fix the change feed and the operations baseline. Each item is small, about a week in total.
2. Put the private beta in front of 8–12 named people and hold 10 discovery conversations.
3. Let what they say pick the first buyer, the coverage plan and the posture.

## 2. Each lens on its own

### 2.1 Product strategy
**Working:**
- Data-rights discipline is enforced in code (`services/api/visibility.py::GatedRecord`, M-11 = 0).
- Measurement is honest: matches are withheld at 0.25; ERCOT "built" was restated from 580 to 117 rather than published as news.
- The risk register and the financial model are good enough to decide on.
- The beta is cheap ($15–35/month).

**Problems:**
- **The evidence gates were skipped.** PRD §6 made 20 conversations an exit criterion for Sprint 2 and a pre-sell an exit criterion for Sprint 3; `docs/50` §4.6 gated the midstream wave on the conversations. None happened (`docs/15` RR-2), and the wave shipped anyway (F).
- **The paid offer was taken apart piece by piece:**
  - the delay was dropped (09-21);
  - paid tiers were made inactive (09-26);
  - alerts were made free (09-30);
  - matches, "the wedge", are withheld;
  - queue coverage is CAISO, ERCOT and NYISO only (F).
- **Scope ran ahead of evidence.** PRD §3.2 put Class VI and gas/LNG/CCS out of scope and §3.4 put midstream Later; all are now built. There are about 197k lines of Python, 157 API operations (61 of them admin) and 36 migrations (F).
- **The PRD is v0.1 of 2026-09-12** and contradicts later owner decisions (F).
- **The metrics measure the pipeline, not users.**
  - M-1 missed its target (7.2 % against ≥ 60 %). It was then redefined against a 600-label set that does not exist (88 labels exist).
  - M-4 to M-8, M-12 and M-13 cannot be measured today (F).
- **The PLAN no longer works as memory.**
  - It is 185 KB, with 127 rows averaging 1,319 characters; about 69 rows are implementation notes.
  - OD-2 and OD-32 are cited but defined nowhere (F).
- **"Sprint 3" means four different things,** so its exit review never happened (F).
- **The launch path is all owner actions,** and it has not moved in 25 days (F).

**Verdict:** "an unusually disciplined data platform with no customer". Freeze scope, put the beta in front of ten named people within a week, and rewrite the PRD around whoever says yes. About 75 % confidence.

**Problems:**

**Verdict:** the real assets point to buyers who need citable diligence (lenders, advisors, gas/CCS and midstream commercial teams) more than to queue-watching developers, whom free tools now serve. Engineering cannot tell the owner who pays.

### 2.4 Architecture
**Working:**
- The import contracts hold (4 kept, 1,701 dependencies).
- Duplication is almost nil (1 near-duplicate group in 3,260 functions).
- The fetch path is sound (a subprocess with an OS timeout).
- There is one visibility predicate.
- The client-IP trust chain is deliberate.

**Problems:**
1. **Nothing limits size.** Lines went from 55,748 to 92,704 in 14 days, and functions of 80+ lines from 48 to 90. Since the 09-26 refactor, `web/app.py` went from 747 to 1,186 lines and `map.js` from 1,469 to 2,328 (F).
2. **`services.api` is the domain kernel,** with imports in both directions with six packages: alerts→api went from 9 to 21 edges, and the licence predicate (927 lines) lives in the HTTP package (F).
3. **Thread timeouts release the store-wide lock while the work keeps writing.** `_run_with_timeout` calls `shutdown(wait=False)`, and no `statement_timeout` is set anywhere (*verified*). This applies to the resolve, enrich, match and context-load jobs, including the two added on 2026-10-09 (I, medium-high).
4. **Single-process assumptions hold the system to one host:**
   - the in-memory rate limiter;
   - per-process indexes at 606–638 MB;
   - local context files;
   - resolve reading parquet rather than the database (F).
5. **Security is below S-2.**
   - No CSP, HSTS or nosniff anywhere (*verified*).
   - 22/80/443 open to 0.0.0.0/0 (*verified*, `infra/terraform/main.tf`).
   - Admin on the apex with a password only.
   - One secrets file in every container.
   - One database role, and no append-only trigger on `event` although `docs/21` §6.1 says there is one (F).
6. **28 of about 3,900 tests run on Postgres,** against 36 dialect branches (F).
7. **Two API contracts, neither generated.** The OpenAPI file is hand-written (15,748 lines), there are 0 Pydantic models, and the CI step called "generated == committed" compares only (method, path) pairs (F).
8. **The ADRs and `docs/20` are stale.** They name four paths that do not exist and controls that were never built (F).

**Verdict:** the system is well-behaved where a contract exists and ungoverned where none does. The documents describe protections that are not there, so reviewers rely on them.

### 2.5 Backend code health
**Working:**
- One `ProblemError` model.
- One keyset paginator (no `.offset(`).
- Enforced layering.
- No mocks, a socket guard, and a core suite that went from 1,572 s to 469 s.
- Hot-path indexes.

**Problems:**
1. **The saved-search feed scans the whole table in Python.** A `q=solar` request takes 6.40 s and 9,570 queries, against 0.11 s and 1 query in SQL. The SQL builder already exists, and feed items are built in four places that disagree (F, measured).
2. **The documented API contract does not exist.** There are 0 `BaseModel`s, and 60 routes take `dict[str, Any]`. Customers are linked to the auto-generated `/redoc`, which has 2 schemas, documents a 422 that is never returned, and is titled "Public tier only (Sprint 2 backend brief)" (F).
3. **Shared helpers are copied, and the copies diverge:**
   - a local `_unique_slug`, contradicting the 10-07 decision;
   - four Crockford decoders;
   - six hand-built 429s;
   - ten UTC helpers (F).
4. **The refactor's gains are gone,** with no ratchet (F).
5. **Two metering paths disagree.** Web-forwarded Pro requests are charged through `charge_credential` (I, moderate-high; needs a test).
6. **There are 21 commits inside loaders,** breaking job atomicity, and `deps.configure()` runs `create_all` on production (F).
7. **19 % of lines are docstrings and comments.** 33 module docstrings run to 40 lines or more, and at least 260 lines narrate "Sprint 2" or "lane X" (F).
8. **Logging falls below E-18:** 59 log calls in 55k lines, and no request log line (F).

**Verdict:** the edges are disciplined and the middle is losing ground. Serve the real spec, make the docs say what is true, and add a ratchet.

### 2.7 Data engineering
**Working:**
- One connector framework with pure parsers and fixtures.
- The unchanged short-circuit and 304s work.
- DQ holds catch real problems.
- Reparse and restatement work.
- Run records act as commit markers.

**Problems:**
1. **Every row that disappears from a source is published as "withdrawn"** (`services/ingest/loader.py:232`, *verified*). EIA-860M completions and grants.gov closures arrive as `removed`. The social and alert paths then announce withdrawals and cancellations with no human step (F).
2. **NESO record keys hash the fields whose changes are the news** (88 % of rows). One date slip becomes new + removed, and no churn gate catches it (F, simulated).
3. **NESO dates are parsed month-first.** `pandas.to_datetime` without `dayfirst` turns `01/07/2033` into 2033-01-07 (*verified*). That is 182 of 1,857 dated rows, 10 % (F).
4. **ERCOT is served one release late.** The tick runs about 16 h before ERCOT publishes, and freshness counts `unchanged` as success. ERCOT is 78 % of active US ISO rows (F for October; I, medium, that it repeats).
5. **Coverage is about a quarter of the US active queue** (2,118 rows / 524 GW against LBNL's ~8,200 / ~2,060 GW), while `docs/02` claims 70–75 %. One US demand source; TED is 81 % of opportunities (F; I on the share).
6. **Volatile envelope bytes defeat the unchanged short-circuit.** Find a Tender's `updatedTo` and the grants.gov JWT trigger 48 needless load→resolve→enrich→match chains a day and write about 113 MB/day (F; I on volume).
7. **The context builders are a second, unmonitored framework.** They re-store unchanged bytes (39 % of 315 MB) (F).
8. **No alert reaches a human,** and FERC's recall is structurally low (F).

**Verdict:** the framework is better than most, but the change feed is not yet trustworthy. All four feed bugs are small fixes to land before any new source. Coverage is the strategic problem, not engineering.

### 2.8 Data science
**Working:**
- Merges are reversible and evented, and members are shown on every page.
- The organisation key was measured properly (a Wilson CI).
- Some out-of-sample evidence exists (H5: 66 records judged; POI crosswalk 39/40).
- Recall is bounded by EIA's coverage, not the matcher (22.5 % link rate).

**Problems:**
1. **The headline 0.974 is in-sample.** It is pairwise, from one labeller, tuned on the same 85 labels, against a 4-week-old fixture. Record-level, pre-H5 wrong merges were 6.8 % (CI 5.0–9.2), so "2–5 %" was low (F).
2. **The labels contradict each other on granularity** (Darden vs Atlas). Darden I–IV are served as one record, and 212 of 543 within-cluster pairs were never scored (F).
3. **No disagreement penalty.** Name plus county alone scores 80, at or above the 75 threshold, with capacity = 0 and COD = 0. 140 accepted pairs have capacity score 0 (F).
4. **The cluster gate's minimum-score check can never fire** (F).
5. **Human decisions do not constrain the next run.** `resolution_decision.status` is never read, so an unmerge survives only by accident (F).
6. **`resolution_confidence` is served** but is just min-score/100: always ≥ 0.75 and uncalibrated (F).
7. **The matcher's score does no work.** Every accepted pair scores 0.865, and all 15 false positives are grants that fund people (F).
8. **The FERC linker's project-level precision is about 20 %** (F).

**Verdict:** the machinery around the resolver is good. "One lifecycle record per project" is not yet a measured property. A flat merge is being forced onto a hierarchy (complex ⊃ phases ⊃ generators, plus re-filings).

### 2.9 Operations, QA, appsec
**Working:**
- A defensive, tested deploy with rollback.
- Releases only from green commits.
- Unusual suite guards (socket guard, ratcheting floors, honest latency tests).
- A cheap beta.

**Problems:**
1. **No alerting, and the health check lies.** `/v1/health` returns 200 with status "degraded" when the database is down (*verified*), and it hard-codes `rate_limiter_active: True` (F).
2. **One stalled job can freeze the pipeline.** Four tasks share `lock="resolve"`; nothing calls Procrastinate's stalled-job recovery; Docker's 10 s stop grace is shorter than a build (I, high that the mechanism exists).
3. **28 of 5,102 tests run on Postgres.** Six Postgres-only defects escaped in ten days (F).
4. **Backups were never restored, and the internet-facing containers hold read-write R2 keys** that can delete them. No dump is taken before migrations (F).
5. **The images shipped are not the images tested.** They are built twice, there is no lock file, the base image is floating despite a comment saying it is digest-pinned, and dev tools and Playwright ship in runtime images (F).
6. **The origin is not hardened:** open ports and no security headers (F).
7. **Exports likely fail in the image.** `EXPORT_DIR` is unset and `/app` is root-owned (I, not executed).
8. **CI and test duplication,** plus an order dependence that was hidden by splitting jobs rather than fixed. Junk is tracked in git: `x.*.log`, an unrelated `lovable-proxy-worker.js`, and 3.46 MB of screenshots (*verified*).

**Verdict:** never operated, and documentation has substituted for feedback (942 lines of `docs/60`, wrong in places, and zero alerts wired). The suite optimises proxies (89 % coverage, 5,100 tests) while the defects come from Postgres, concurrency and process lifecycle.

**Working:**

  - an unreachable meter;
  - `/v1/me` reporting wrong limits;
  - a privacy link that returned 404;
  - document drift.
- **The guardrails held:** no model names, agents never commit, gitleaks, import contracts.

**Problems:**
1. **The role mix is skewed toward building.** CHANGELOG attribution: coordinator 97, backend 46, data-engineer 35, frontend 21, data-scientist 17; against product-manager 5, market-researcher 3, qa-engineer 2, sales-bd 2 (F, `docs/CHANGELOG.md`). The loops that would have stopped scope creep (PM gate, QA release gate, sales evidence) are dormant. That is the mechanism behind §2.1's "scope ran ahead of evidence".
2. **Project memory is too heavy to read and too central to merge.**
   - Docs total 2.48 MB: PLAN 185 KB; CHANGELOG 386 KB with 1,512-character entries; `docs/22` 242 KB.
   - Every agent is told to read the PLAN first, and lane agents used 200–400k tokens each.
   - PLAN and CHANGELOG conflict in every parallel lane; the owner's own 10-09 instruction expected exactly that (F).
3. **The agent definitions are stale.**
   - `backend-developer` still enforces "tier enforcement (delayed vs live)", although the delay was removed on 09-21.
   - `docs/03` §5 says "Sprint 0 (now)".
   - QA's "release gate" and the supervision Routine (`docs/03` §1) were never exercised (F).
4. **Lanes add, nothing removes.**
   - Path allowlists leave leftovers: lane D could not delete the meter it made unreachable.
   - Briefs built from stale docs waste work: lane C's axe fixes already existed.
   - Lanes do not see each other's open items: lane A listed a gap lane B had just fixed (F, this session).

5. **Owner decisions pile up faster than they are answered** (at least 32). Each lane adds questions without a recommended default (F, `docs/00-PLAN.md`).
6. **Merge mechanics lose work.**
   - #67 merged without its second commit (a merge against a stale head).
   - Branch names (`sprint-3-attio-adapter-*`) carried unrelated work for weeks.
   - 24 commits on main carry model names in trailers (OD-2, still open) (F).

**Verdict:** the team is an excellent build engine with no brake and no steering wheel. The fix is not more agents. It is three process changes:
- a product gate in front of every lane;
- a QA/review gate after every integration;
- memory that is small, current and conflict-free.

## 3. Synthesis

### 3.1 Where the lenses converge
1. **Learning is the binding constraint, not code.** Product, market, legal and orchestration agree on this. The owner's time is the scarce input, and each build wave consumes more of it with new decisions.
2. **The documents describe a system that does not exist.** All nine lenses found a version of this:
   - a stale PRD;
   - a generated contract that is not generated;
   - 2FA, database roles and a Postgres limiter that do not exist;
   - an append-only trigger that does not exist;
   - "Docker restarts unhealthy containers";
   - a "digest-pinned" base image that is not pinned;
   - "70–75 % coverage";
   - "anonymised" for data that is pseudonymised;
   - agent definitions that enforce a delay removed on 09-21.

   This is the most dangerous finding, because agents and reviewers act on the documents.
3. **The hard-to-retrofit core is good.** Licence gating, provenance, reversible merges, the error model, pagination, import contracts, deploy tooling. Nobody recommends rebuilding any of it.
4. **The product's core output has correctness bugs** that no gate catches: false "withdrawn" events, NESO keys and dates, ERCOT a month late, uncalibrated `resolution_confidence`, human decisions ignored by the next run. These outrank every refactor.
5. **Growth has no guardrails.** There is no size ratchet. Testing runs on the wrong database. Single-process assumptions are baked in. `map.js` grew 3.7×. The 09-26 refactor has been undone.
6. **The security baseline is missing at the origin:** headers, firewall, an admin second factor, secrets blast radius, backups deletable from the API container.

### 3.2 Where the lenses disagree
- **Freeze versus breadth.** Product and market say freeze scope. The counter-argument: you chose breadth-first, noncommercial, "maximum data access, then decide", and agent breadth is cheap. The lenses agree that this stops holding once owner attention is the constraint, and once the breadth went to context layers rather than the PJM/MISO coverage a queue buyer judges first.
- **Which buyer.**
  - Product frames the choice as developers and lenders (who need PJM/MISO) versus midstream/CCS/powered-land operators (what has been built).
  - Market proposes a Texas/ERCOT wedge, or diligence buyers.
  - Data engineering offers a coverage route that needs no licence: federal power-marketing-administration and TVA queues, SAM.gov, DOE eXCHANGE.
  - Only conversations can settle this.
- **Refactor timing.** Architecture and backend want kernel extraction and Pydantic. Product wants a work-in-progress limit; operations wants proportion. **Resolution:** do the small guardrails now (ratchet, headers, alerting, stalled jobs, feed fixes). Defer the large refactors until there is buyer evidence.
- **Posture.** Market and legal: noncommercial forfeits revenue for about 18 rows and is hard to defend. Product: the owner chose it deliberately. All three agree the day-30 review cannot be decided on its stated inputs, because nothing is deployed.

### 3.3 Challenges to the owner's framing
- **"Keep going" has been read as "build more".** It produced four more feature lanes this week. The review says the next unit of progress is a conversation, not a lane. Part of this session's own work (daily caps for a product with no users) is the kind of breadth the review warns against. The retention job and in-app deletion were legally necessary before a public opening.
- **Sprint numbers hide the fact that the exit criteria were never met.** Dated milestones would not: beta open, ten conversations held, first buyer named, public open.
- **Asking "what would experts say"** has an answer, and they agree: the engineering is ahead of the business. More expertise applied to the build will not change that.
