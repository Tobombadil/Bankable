Prices

What power sells for at the main trading hubs and load zones, averaged over the last twelve months where the history allows, and what businesses pay on average in each state.

Wholesale price (LMP)
The market price of electricity at a place on the grid: set the day before for each hour of the next day in the day-ahead market, and every few minutes as the power flows in real time.
Basis
How far a location's price sits above or below its market's reference hub on average; a negative basis means power sells for less there.
Negative-price hours
The share of hours priced below zero, when there is more power nearby than the grid can carry away; a generator earns less, a battery or a flexible load can be paid to use power.
Daily spread
The average gap between each day's four highest and four lowest hourly prices: a rough ceiling on what a four-hour battery could earn moving energy, before losses.

Each statistic covers the trailing twelve months ending with the last complete operating day, or as much of them as the history held here allows; a figure resting on under 90% of the intervals its window should hold is not shown. How these figures are worked out.

How these figures are worked out

  • Averages are the plain mean of every interval in the window (every interval of a market has the same length), not weighted by load. The window is the trailing twelve months to the last complete operating day; where less history is held, the window column says so and a short window is marked in its cell.
  • Day-ahead and real-time are each market's own: ERCOT's real-time prices are 15-minute settlement point prices, SPP's 5-minute (read once an hour), CAISO's the 15-minute pre-dispatch market (RTPD), NYISO's the hourly integrated price, and Great Britain's the half-hourly system (imbalance) price, which has no day-ahead counterpart.
  • Basis is the location's price minus its market's reference hub's, interval by interval, averaged; NYISO has no reference hub here, so it has no basis.
  • Negative-price hours are the share of the window's intervals priced below zero.
  • Daily spread is each complete day's four highest hourly prices less its four lowest (real-time prices averaged to the hour first), averaged over the days.
  • Sorting: the last-30-days, basis, negative and spread columns show the day-ahead figure where a market has one and the real-time one for Great Britain, and sort on what they show. Pound prices sort after dollar prices, never between them.
